# Grammar sequence and the traps inside it

Every vocabulary row carries both an English and a French gloss — see the
"Every word is glossed twice" section in SKILL.md. The French one is where his
interference shows up, so the traps below that mention French are the ones to
flag hardest.

Ordered by week. Everything here has a row in the Notion Gramática database —
update the row, don't duplicate it. The "trap" column is what to listen for.

## Weeks 1–4 · the tense system

**Presente** — regular endings, then the three stem changes (e→ie *pienso*,
o→ue *puedo*, e→i *pido*). Nosotros and vosotros never change. Yo-irregulars:
*hago, pongo, salgo, traigo, conozco, conduzco, doy, sé, veo*.
*Trap:* over-using subject pronouns. *Yo trabajo, yo vivo, yo tengo* sounds foreign.

**Ser vs. estar** — ser = identity/definition; estar = state/location/result.
The meaning-shifting adjectives matter more than the list: *es aburrido* (boring)
vs *está aburrido* (bored); same for *listo, rico, verde, malo, bueno*.
*Trap:* French *je suis 38 ans* → *tengo 38 años*.

**Indefinido** — regulars, then the strong stems as one family sharing endings
(-e, -iste, -o, -imos, -isteis, -ieron, **no accents**): *tuv-, estuv-, pud-,
pus-, sup-, hub-, anduv-, hic-* (¡**hizo**!), *quis-, vin-, dij-, traj-*.
Verbs in -j drop the i: *dijeron*, not *dijieron*. *Ser* and *ir* share *fui*.
Spelling shifts in yo: *busqué, llegué, empecé*.
*Trap:* putting accents on the strong preterites (*tuvó* ✗).

**Imperfecto** — only three irregulars in the entire language: *era, iba, veía*.

**The contrast** — teach it as camera work, not as a rule list. Imperfecto = the
set, camera still, describing/repeating. Indefinido = the action, camera moving,
something happens and ends. Together: *Llovía cuando salí.*
Meaning-shifting pairs: *sabía*/*supe*, *conocía*/*conocí*, *quería*/*quise*
(and *no quise* = refused), *podía*/*pude*.

**Perfecto** — *haber* + participle, never separated, participle never agrees.
Irregulars: *hecho, dicho, visto, puesto, vuelto, escrito, abierto, roto,
muerto, cubierto, resuelto, satisfecho*.
*Spain-specific:* unfinished time periods take the perfect — *hoy he ido*,
*esta semana he trabajado mucho*. This is a strong accent marker; lean into it.

**Pluscuamperfecto** — *había* + participle.

## Weeks 5–8 · precision

**Pronouns** — OD *lo/la/los/las*, OI *le/les*. Together, OI first, and *le/les*
becomes **se**: *se lo di*, never *le lo*. Placement: before the conjugated verb,
attached to infinitive/gerund/affirmative imperative. IO doubling is obligatory:
*Le di el libro a Marta*. Peninsular leísmo (*le vi* for a man) is accepted by
the RAE for masculine singular persons only.

**Por / para** — para is the forward arrow (destination, purpose, deadline,
recipient, opinion); por is cause, route, exchange, duration, agent.
The clarifying pair: *lo hago por mi madre* (for her sake) vs *para mi madre*
(to give her). Fixed-preposition verbs are memorised as blocks: *soñar con,
contar con, quedar en, insistir en, tratar de, acordarse de, depender de,
empezar a, atreverse a, casarse con, pensar en*.

**Presente de subjuntivo** — built from the yo form, vowel swapped (-ar takes e,
-er/-ir take a), so yo-irregulars carry over (*tengo→tenga*). Six true
irregulars: *sea, vaya, esté, dé, sepa, haya*. Triggers, in teaching order:
wish/influence (*quiero que*), evaluation (*es importante que*), doubt/negated
belief (*no creo que* — but *creo que* + indicative), future time clauses
(*cuando llegues*), purpose (*para que*). The two-subject rule governs
infinitive vs *que* + subjunctive.

**Imperativo** — affirmative tú = 3rd person present, with eight irregulars
(*di, haz, ve, pon, sal, sé, ten, ven*). Vosotros = infinitive with -d
(*hablad*), and the d drops before *os* (*sentaos, callaos, iros*). Negative is
**always** subjunctive. Pronouns attach in the affirmative, precede in the negative.

**Futuro / condicional** — shared irregular stems: *tendr-, podr-, saldr-,
pondr-, vendr-, dir-, har-, querr-, sabr-, habr-, cabr-, valdr-*. Both express
probability: *estará en el bar* (present guess), *serían las tres* (past guess).

## Weeks 9–12 · nuance

**Imperfecto de subjuntivo** — mechanical from the 3rd person plural preterite
minus -ron, plus -ra or -se. Zero irregulars of its own. Structure: `si +
imperfecto de subjuntivo + condicional`. The conditional **never** follows *si*.
*Como si* always takes it.

**Pluscuamperfecto de subjuntivo + condicional compuesto** — third conditional
(*si hubiera sabido, habría dicho*; in Spain *hubiera... hubiera* is also
correct and common), mixed conditionals, and *debería/podría/tendría que haber*
for regret.

**Connectors** — three per function, drilled to automaticity: add, contrast,
cause, consequence, concession, reformulate, close. *Aunque* + indicative = fact,
+ subjunctive = hypothesis or irrelevance. Relative clauses put the preposition
**before** the relative (*la chica de la que te hablé*). *Cuyo* agrees with the
thing possessed. Queísmo/dequeísmo test: substitute *eso*.

**The six 'se'** — reflexive, reciprocal, pasiva refleja (agrees), impersonal
(always singular), accidental (*se me olvidó* — verb agrees with the thing),
aspectual (*me leí el libro entero*). Plus the verbs that change meaning:
*ir/irse, dormir/dormirse, quedar/quedarse, acordar/acordarse, llevar/llevarse,
poner/ponerse*.

## Weeks 13–16 · fluency

**Vosotros** in every tense, plus *os* and *vuestro*. **Distinción**: c before
e/i and z as English *th*; the apical Spanish *s*; relaxed final -d
(*Madri(z)*); harsher *j*.

**Perífrasis** — *llevar + gerundio* (the fix for the French *depuis* calque),
*llevar sin + inf*, *acabar de*, *ponerse a*, *dejar de*, *volver a* (the
Spanish "again"), *soler*, *seguir + gerundio*, *estar a punto de*,
*andar + gerundio*.

**The five 'become'** — *ponerse* (fast, temporary, emotional), *quedarse*
(result, often a loss), *volverse* (deep character change), *hacerse*
(gradual, willed), *llegar a ser* (end of a long process), *convertirse en*
(radical transformation).

**Register** — usted (rarer in Spain than in America), conditional and imperfect
subjunctive for politeness (*quisiera*, *¿podría?*, *¿sería posible que...?*),
avoiding *ser* + participle in favour of the active or *se*, and the French
false-friend set.

**Compensation strategies** — *es una cosa que sirve para..., es como un... pero
más..., no me sale la palabra pero es cuando...* Plus native fillers: *bueno,
pues, o sea, es que, la verdad es que, hombre, a ver, en plan*. Switching to
English or French is the one thing that is never allowed.
