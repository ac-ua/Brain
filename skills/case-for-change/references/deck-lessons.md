# Deck lessons

Generic lessons from client decks (Aug–Oct 2026). Account-specific context stays on the opportunity pages.

## Story
- **One storyline**, stated in a sentence before any slide; every slide serves it. Decks rejected by the team had
  two stories fighting (exec value vs technical tour).
- **Action titles**: the title says the conclusion ("Claims wait 9 days because three systems don't talk"), not
  the topic ("Current state").
- **Their architecture first**: draw the client's systems and process before ours; then show where we sit.
- **Not a feature list**: proof by outcome, one platform capability per pain.
- **Short**: about 30% shorter than the first draft; audit and internal content stay out of client decks.
- **The deck says what it's about**: when the client asked for a case for change, don't drift into the POC plan.

## Facts
- **Product-accurate names only**: the platform's real module and feature names; no invented capability.
- **Every number has a source** (client figure / our model / validated); unconfirmed ones get a yellow
  `[to be validated]` footnote.
- **No claim nobody asked for**: no success metric or commitment the client or the AE didn't raise.
- **Screenshots anonymised** (no other client's data, no internal URLs).

## Format
- Ask which theme (client or UnifyApps) before building.
- Use `unifyapps-brand-deck` (team plugin, Playbook v4) when installed; it wins over `unifyapps-format`.
- Converting to Google Slides: check fonts and spacing after the import.
- Competitive decks: ≤ 3 content slides, a point of view ("AI in the box vs across the boxes"), not a comparison grid.
- Render every slide and look at it before sending; fix overflow and orphan lines.
