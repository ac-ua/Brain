# CEP Workbook - tab reference

Source of truth for the master template: `https://docs.google.com/document/d/1Kr61vmC8Ji3VldN1nAWbEDaMvoB76xCTallEWc3iJ-s`. If this reference ever seems out of sync with the live template (a tab renamed, added, or removed), re-read the template with the Drive `read_file_content` tool and treat the live doc as authoritative.

Each tab below is listed with its built-in definition (the italic text that lives under the tab heading in the doc - keep it in place, don't overwrite it) and a note on what kind of content goes there and in what style.

## Process tabs (usually leave alone)

- **Client Workbook Checklist** - a stage-exit checklist (EDUCATE / ENGAGE / LEARN / SHOW / ALIGN / TECHNICAL WIN / BUSINESS WIN / PAPER WIN / CLOSED-WON / DELIVER / GROW). This tracks sales-process stage, not deal content. Leave untouched unless the user explicitly asks to check off a stage.
- **General Notes** - freeform catch-all. Only use for real, grounded material that doesn't cleanly belong to any topic tab below. Don't use it as a dumping ground for things you're unsure about - if it's not grounded, it doesn't belong here either.

## Narrative tabs (write as prose paragraphs)

- **Challenge/Educate** - "Understanding the customer's market context, core needs, and buying journey. Helps customers clearly prioritize their own use cases while grounding AI possibilities in their specific business realities." Company background, market pressures, internal initiatives, and how we're positioning/educating them.
- **RFP** - "How to evaluate and navigate the RFP process strategically. Covers understanding how the RFP was created, assessing whether it favors a competitor, and determining when to engage or disengage based on conviction and influence." Only fill if there is an actual RFP in play.
- **Need** - "Validating and qualifying the customer's stated need. Ensures the problem is real, prioritized, and tied to measurable business impact before advancing."
- **Value** - "Confirms the customer's ability to recognize and articulate value. The customer must clearly see how the solution delivers meaningful business impact."
- **Current State** - "A shared, detailed understanding of the customer's current environment. Captures systems, processes, constraints, and inefficiencies as they exist today."
- **Negative Consequences** - "The risks, costs, and consequences of maintaining the current state. Clarifies what happens if the problem is not addressed."
- **Future State** - "The customer's desired future state after the problem is solved. How people, processes, and technology should operate post-transformation."
- **Positive Business Outcomes** - "Quantifies the tangible business outcomes the customer expects to achieve. Ties the future state to measurable results and success metrics."
- **Required Capabilities** - "Captures the capabilities required to achieve the desired outcomes. Ensures alignment on what the solution must deliver functionally and technically."
- **How we do it** - "Explains how our solution addresses the customer's needs and required capabilities."
- **How we do it better** - "Highlights our differentiation and unique advantages vs. alternatives."
- **Proof Points** - "Validates credibility through relevant customer examples and results."
- **Implicated Pain** - "Elevates the problem above background noise. Ensures the customer feels real urgency and risk if the issue is not solved."
- **Procurement Process** - "Procurement steps and policies. How vendors are evaluated, negotiated, and approved."
- **Legal Process and Requirements** - "Legal steps, requirements, and constraints."
- **Finance Process and Requirements** - "Finance approvals, controls, and evaluation criteria."
- **Signature Process** - "How final approval and signature occur. Identifies signatories and sequencing."
- **Implementation** - "Plans and executes delivery against the agreed scope."
- **Growth** - "Expansion opportunities beyond the initial scope. White space and a structured growth plan."

## Structured tabs (bold labels + bullets - people, numbers, dates)

- **Budget** - "Confirms that funding exists and is accessible. Budget must be qualified, aligned to the initiative, and validated against pricing."
- **Priority/Timing** - "Whether the initiative is a true business priority and when action will occur. Urgency, sequencing, decision timelines."
- **Metrics** - "How success will be measured."
- **Economic Buyer** - "Identifies and secures engagement with the Economic Buyer. Document their name, title, reporting line, decision authority, and LinkedIn profile." Example style (from a real filled workbook):
  > **Name:** Josh Langley
  > **Role:** Chief Information Officer (CIO)
  > **Linkedin:** <https://www.linkedin.com/in/jlangley/>
  > **Credibility and Position within the Organization:** As the CIO of Iron Mountain, Josh holds ultimate authority over the enterprise's technology stack...
  > **Motivation & Background:**
  > - The "Agentic" Vision: He strongly desires to transition the company into an "agentic enterprise"...
- **Decision Criteria** - "Documents the criteria used to evaluate and select a solution."
- **Decision Process** - "Maps the steps required to reach a decision. Approvals, sequencing, and dependencies."
- **Decision Committee** (parent/checklist tab) - role checklist across AI, IT, and Business committees. Put role names as bullets here at most; put the actual person + LinkedIn detail in the matching child tab below.
  - **AI Decision Committee** - AI Governance, Head of Data, Data Sovereignty Owner, AI/ML Architecture Lead, Compliance/Risk - name + LinkedIn per role.
  - **IT Decision Committee** - Cloud Architect, Security Lead/CISO, Org Infrastructure, Lead Enterprise Architect, IT Procurement - name + LinkedIn per role.
  - **Business Decision Committee** - Business Leader (Function Owner), Budget Holder, Operations Lead, Transformation Lead - name + LinkedIn per role.
- **Champion** - "Building and validating a strong internal champion. Document the champion's name, role, credibility within the organization, motivation, and LinkedIn profile." Example style:
  > Hari Ramakrishnan (CDO). [Linkedin](...)
  > **Role:** Lead for AI Strategy and Roadmap Execution / Executive Sponsor for Commercial Excellence.
  > **Credibility within the Organization:** Hari possesses high influence and strategic authority...
  > **Motivation:**
  > - Eliminating the IT Bottleneck: ...
- **Coaches** - "Identifies and enables internal coaches who provide insight and guidance. Capture names, roles, access points, and LinkedIn profiles."
- **Detractors** - "Identifies individuals opposed to the initiative. Identify by name, role, influence level, objections, and LinkedIn profile."
- **Competition** - "Assesses competitive alternatives and tactics. Identifies traps set by competitors and defines counter-strategies."
- **Partners** (parent/checklist tab) - summary only; detail goes in child tabs.
  - **Services Partner** - who delivers implementation/services - names, roles, responsibilities, LinkedIn.
  - **Cloud/Tech Partner** - hosting/infra/tech dependencies - names, roles, responsibilities, LinkedIn.

## Notes on scope

This is a MEDDPICC-style enterprise sales account-planning workbook (Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria/Process, Identified Pain, Champion, Competition) merged with a value-selling framework (Current State / Negative Consequences / Future State / Positive Business Outcomes) and post-sale planning (Implementation, Growth). When mapping a transcript or deck to tabs, think in those terms: is this fact about the buying process, about a person's role/influence, about the customer's pain, or about the value story - each maps to a different tab.
