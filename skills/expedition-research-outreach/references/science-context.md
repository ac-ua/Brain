## Why a science angle (framing for every email)

The expedition is a rare data-collection platform, and its solo, unsupported traveller
is himself a case that laboratories cannot reproduce in controlled conditions. There is
an established scientific precedent for exactly this type of crossing: SPEAR-17, Ice Maiden
and Inspire-22 (pre/post calorimetry and hypoxic-chamber testing), a 93-day solo crossing
studied via dried blood spots as a space analog, and the British Antarctic Survey's 2025
first mapping of microplastics in interior Antarctic snow.

## CRITICAL difference from the sponsor skill — no financial lead

This is NOT a sponsorship ask. The value exchange is reversed: Aurélien OFFERS rare
access, samples, and data; in return he seeks a scientific collaboration and the
credibility of an institutional partnership (which also strengthens the sponsorship
side of the project). Never lead with money. Never ask the lab to pay, and never
frame the email around funding. If a lab happens to have funding capacity, that can
surface later in a call. The primary ask is always: a short exchange to see if a
collaboration makes sense.

## The three research axes (priority: 2× Axe 1, then 1× Axe 2, then 1× Axe 3, repeat)

### Axe 1 — Environnement : prélèvements de neige (PRIORITY)

- **Fit:** the Hercules Inlet → South Pole transect is a ground sampling line that labs
  almost never get to traverse. Two analyses fit in one gesture (scoop surface snow into a
  clean bottle at GPS points): microplastics, and black carbon / impurities.
- **Light protocol (what Aurélien brings):** pre-cleaned bottles provided by the lab,
  surface-snow samples at regular GPS points, with GPS/time/temperature/wind noted.
  The cold preserves samples on its own. Minimal weight and effort.
- **Point to raise proactively:** his own synthetic clothing is a microplastics
  contamination source, so the lab's clean protocol (gloves, dedicated bottles, precise
  gestures) is mandatory. Mentioning it shows he understands the constraint.
- **Value to the lab:** fills a real data gap in remote inland Antarctica at near-zero
  cost to the lab.

### Axe 2 — Corps humain : physiologie, nutrition, froid

- **Fit:** the solo unsupported traveller is a rare subject, and a dedicated scientific
  pipeline for this type of traverse already exists.
- **Light protocol (what Aurélien brings):**
  1. Passive: continuous data from a multisport watch (HR, HRV, sleep, SpO2, skin temp),
     already worn. Synergy with a Garmin-type partner.
  2. Pre/post in a lab: body composition, VO2max, blood markers, cognitive tests.
     Zero field burden.
  3. Optional on the ice: quick food log, and a few dried blood spots ONLY if a lab
     really judges it scientifically worthwhile. Accept only if the value is real.
- **Timing reality:** human-subject studies usually need ethics approval (several months).
  Prefer labs that already have a validated expedition protocol so Aurélien can be added
  as a subject rather than launching a brand-new study before November.
- **Value to the lab:** a rare solo data point that extends an existing published series.

### Axe 3 — Isolement / analogue spatial

- **Fit:** Concordia is a recognised space-analog for isolation, but always in a team
  configuration. A full solo traverse, with no possibility of stopping, is a more extreme
  case and a data point station studies cannot produce. ICE-environment research already
  includes polar treks among its subjects.
- **Light protocol (what Aurélien brings):** a short structured self-report folded into
  the daily audio dispatches he already sends (mood, sleep, perceived load); short
  cognitive tests on his phone pre/post and occasionally en route; sleep and rest-activity
  rhythm from the watch (relevant with 24h daylight of the austral summer).
- **Value to the lab:** a unique solo ICE subject with continuous self-report.

## Light-protocol menu (hard boundary — never exceed)

Acceptable: passive watch data; pre/post lab measures; a handful of snow bottles; a 2-minute
structured audio note already part of the daily dispatch; optionally a few dried blood spots
if a lab insists and the value is clear. Never propose: heavy equipment, daily protocols that
eat into skiing hours, or anything that could jeopardise the expedition's success. Doing
science does not break the "unsupported / unassisted" status as long as Aurélien carries
everything himself. Reassure the researcher of this in the email when relevant.
